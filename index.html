Linear Algebra: The Space of Data

The stage where Deep Learning happens. Understanding dimensions, transformations, and bases.

I. Fundamentals & Data Representation

Scalars, Vectors & Matrices

  • Scalar ($x \in \mathbb{R}$): A single number (e.g., Learning Rate).
  • Vector ($v \in \mathbb{R}^n$): An array of numbers. Represents a point in space or a direction.
    $$ v = [x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n]^T $$
  • Matrix ($A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$): A 2D array. Represents a linear map or a dataset.

The Dot Product (Similarity)

The most important operation in DL. It measures alignment between two vectors.

$$ a \cdot b = ||a|| ||b|| \cos(\theta) = \sum a_i b_i $$

If $a \cdot b = 0$, they are Orthogonal (uncorrelated).

II. Linear Combinations & Span

The Language of Vectors

Most operations are just adding scaled vectors:

$$ w = c_1 \vec{v}_1 + c_2 \vec{v}_2 + \dots $$

The Span is the set of all possible points you can reach by tweaking $c_1, c_2$.

Independence & Basis

  • Dependent: You can build one vector using the others (redundant).
  • Basis: A minimal set of independent vectors that spans the whole space.

IV. Matrix Operations as Transformations

A Neural Network layer $y = \sigma(Wx + b)$ is just a linear transformation followed by a non-linearity.

Matrix-Vector Multiplication

Matrix $W$ transforms vector $x$ (rotate, scale, shear).

$$ y = Wx $$

Think of columns of $W$ as the new basis vectors for the output space.

Determinant

$\det(W)$ measures how the transformation changes volume.

  • $\det(W) = 2$: Area doubles.
  • $\det(W) = 0$: Dimension collapse (Information lost!).

Calculus: The Engine of Optimization

Training a neural network is just minimizing a function. Calculus tells us which way to move.

I. Multivariable Calculus Fundamentals

Partial Derivatives

How does the function change if we wiggle only one input variable $x_i$?

$$ \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_i} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x_1, \dots, x_i+h, \dots) - f(x)}{h} $$

The Gradient ($\nabla f$)

A vector collecting all partial derivatives. It points to the steepest ascent.

$$ \nabla f = [\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_1}, \dots, \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_n}]^T $$

Update Rule: $\theta_{new} = \theta_{old} - \eta \nabla L$

II. Matrices of Derivatives

The Jacobian ($J$)

For vector-valued functions $f: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^m$ (like a layer output), the derivative is a matrix.

$$ J_{ij} = \frac{\partial y_i}{\partial x_j} $$

Backprop: Multiplying Jacobians layer-by-layer.

The Hessian ($H$)

The matrix of second derivatives (Curvature).

$$ H_{ij} = \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_i \partial x_j} $$

Tells us if the critical point is a Minima, Maxima, or Saddle Point.

V. Advanced Operators: Laplacian & Hessian

The Laplacian ($\nabla^2 f$)

The Divergence of the Gradient. It measures how much a value differs from the average of its neighbors.

$ \nabla^2 f = \sum \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_i^2} = \text{Tr}(H) $

Intuition: Diffusion. If $\nabla^2 f$ is high, the function is "spiky" and wants to smooth out.

Connection to Hessian

The Laplacian is just the Trace (sum of diagonal elements) of the Hessian matrix.

While Hessian gives full curvature shape (saddle, valley), Laplacian gives the "average curvature".

VII. Integration & Volume

Riemann Sums

Integration is summing infinite slices. Area under a curve.

$$ \int_a^b f(x)dx \approx \sum f(x_i) \Delta x $$

3D Volume Integrals

Summing voxels to find the mass or volume of a 3D shape.

$$ \iiint_V f(x,y,z) dV $$

DL Context: Probability normalization (ensuring $\int P(x)dx = 1$) over high-dimensional spaces.

Probability: Modeling Uncertainty

The language of uncertainty. Random Variables, Distributions, and Information.

I. Foundations: The Basics

Random Variable ($X$)

A variable whose value is subject to chance. It maps outcomes to numbers.

  • Discrete: Countable steps (e.g., Coin flips). Uses PMF.
  • Continuous: Smooth range (e.g., Temperature). Uses PDF.

Functions of Probability

  • PMF/PDF ($P(x)$): How likely is a specific value?
  • CDF ($F(x)$): Probability of being $\le x$. Accumulates the PMF.
  • Expectation ($E[X]$): The weighted average (mean).

II. Distribution Zoo (Interactive)

Visualize how parameters change the shape of common distributions.

Distribution

III. Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE)

The bridge between Statisics and Optimization.

$$ \theta^* = \arg\max_\theta \prod_{i=1}^N P(x_i | \theta) $$

We convert the product to a sum using $\log$ (monotonicity) to get the Negative Log Likelihood (NLL) loss.

$$ L(\theta) = -\sum \log P(x_i | \theta) $$

Minimizing NLL is equivalent to minimizing Cross-Entropy.

IV. Information Theory

Entropy ($H$)

The minimum number of bits needed to encode a message from distribution $P$. Measures uncertainty.

$$ H(P) = -\mathbb{E}_{x \sim P}[\log P(x)] $$

KL Divergence ($D_{KL}$)

The "distance" between two probability distributions $P$ (True) and $Q$ (Predicted).

$$ D_{KL}(P || Q) = \sum P(x) \log \frac{P(x)}{Q(x)} $$

Tensors: The Language of Frameworks

How PyTorch and TensorFlow actually see the world. Beyond Matrices.

I. What is a Tensor?

Definition

A multi-dimensional array of numerical data. A generalization of scalars and matrices.

  • Rank 0: Scalar (e.g., Loss)
  • Rank 1: Vector (e.g., Bias)
  • Rank 2: Matrix (e.g., Weights)
  • Rank 3: RGB Image ($C \times H \times W$)
  • Rank 4: Batch ($B \times C \times H \times W$)

Views & Strides

In memory, a Tensor is just a 1D array. "Shapes" are just views defined by Strides (how many steps to skip to get to the next element).

reshape() is often free ($O(1)$) because it just changes the stride, not the data.

II. Operations & Broadcasting

Broadcasting Rules

How do we add `(32, 10)` + `(10)`? Frameworks "stretch" the smaller tensor.

  1. Align dimensions from the right.
  2. A dimension is compatible if they are equal OR one of them is 1.
$$ A_{ij} + b_j \implies A_{ij} + \text{copy}(b_j) $$

III. Einstein Summation (einsum)

The "Swiss Army Knife" of tensor operations. Define any operation by labeling indices.

Matrix Multiplication

$$ C_{ik} = \sum_j A_{ij} B_{jk} $$

Code: einsum('ij,jk->ik', A, B)

Batch Matrix Multiplication

$$ C_{bij} = \sum_k A_{bik} B_{bkj} $$

Code: einsum('bik,bkj->bij', A, B)

IV. Tensor Calculus & Backprop

How do we find gradients of shapes? We match dimensions.

Example: Linear Layer ($Y = XW$)

Forward: $(N, D) \times (D, H) \to (N, H)$

Goal: Find $\frac{\partial L}{\partial W}$. Shape must match $W$ ($D, H$).

The Gradient Rule

The gradient is the input transposed times the output gradient.

$$ \frac{\partial L}{\partial W} = X^T \cdot \frac{\partial L}{\partial Y} $$

Dim Check: $(D, N) \times (N, H) \to (D, H)$. Matches!

Optimization: Navigating the Landscape

How do we find the bottom of a high-dimensional valley? The evolution of gradient descent.

I. Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD)

The simplest approach: move in the direction opposite to the gradient.

$$ \theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - \eta \nabla L(\theta_t) $$

The Problem: If the loss surface is a "narrow ravine," SGD oscillates wildly across the steep sides while making slow progress toward the minima.

II. Momentum: Adding Inertia

Inspired by physics. We accumulate a "velocity" $v$ to dampen oscillations and accelerate in consistent directions.

$$ v_{t+1} = \gamma v_t + \eta \nabla L(\theta_t) $$ $$ \theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - v_{t+1} $$

Intuition: A heavy ball rolling down a hill. It gains speed in the direction of the long-term trend and ignores small bumps.

III. Adaptive Methods (RMSProp & Adam)

RMSProp

Normalizes the gradient by a running average of its magnitude. Prevents gradients from exploding or vanishing in specific dimensions.

$$ E[g^2]_t = 0.9 E[g^2]_{t-1} + 0.1 g_t^2 $$

Adam (The Standard)

Combines Momentum (mean) and RMSProp (variance).

$$ \theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - \frac{\eta \cdot \text{m}_t}{\sqrt{\text{v}_t} + \epsilon} $$

Deep Intuition: Why Adam is the "King"

Adam effectively gives every single weight its own personalized learning rate. Weights that have sparse or noisy gradients get their updates boosted, while weights in steep ravines get their updates dampened for stability.

Scientific Debugging Methodology

1. Investigation

Never guess. Always inspect the data. Look at batch composition, gradient norms, and loss curves.

2. Hypothesis

Formulate a specific claim. e.g., "The gradients are vanishing because the activation function saturates."

3. Derivation

Prove it on paper (or in our math view). If the math doesn't support the hypothesis, the hypothesis is wrong.

4. Application

Change one variable at a time to validate the fix.
